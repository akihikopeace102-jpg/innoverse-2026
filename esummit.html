The world

Built around building ventures.

Through startup challenges, business simulations, pitching competitions, and expert interactions, participants work through the full cycle of entrepreneurship — from spotting a problem to pitching a solution.

E-Summit tests whether you can turn an idea into a strategy, and a strategy into results. Every event forces a decision, and every decision has a consequence.

01

E-Summit · Business Strategy

The Brand Reboot

The Brand Reboot puts you in charge of a discontinued brand. Through strategic bidding, product reinvention, and a business pitch, teams compete to prove they can turn a dying brand into a viable one.

The journey begins with Round 1: The Auction, where teams compete to acquire discontinued brands using virtual currency. With a fixed budget in hand, teams must bid wisely, balancing ambition with strategy. The highest bidders secure their chosen brands and earn their place in the next round.

Then comes Round 2: The Reboot, where teams get 5 minutes to give their acquired brand a new direction. Teams must identify what consumers need and develop product upgrades or new offerings that make the brand relevant again.

The final verdict will be based on creativity, originality, innovation, and presentation.

Teams may contain 3–5 students from Classes 9–12. Participants are expected to bring their laptops and cell phones.

Teams of 3–5

02

E-Summit · Pitching

Pitch Arena

Pitch Arena challenges you to identify a real problem, build a solution, and present it in a clear, structured pitch. It tests whether you can explain your idea in a way that makes others want to invest in it.

The journey begins with Round 1: The Idea. Start with a problem that feels real to you. Identify the challenge, think of a creative solution, and shape it into a clear business idea.

Then comes Round 2: The Pitch, where participants get 5–7 minutes to present their problem, solution, target audience, unique value proposition, business model, competitors, and future potential. A prototype or demo can also be showcased to bring the idea to life.

The final verdict will be based on creativity, originality, innovation, problem–solution fit, feasibility, impact, and presentation. The focus is not just on how impressive an idea sounds, but on how well it solves a genuine problem and how convincingly participants bring their vision to life.

Participants can compete individually or in teams of up to 4 students. Participants are expected to bring their laptops, cell phones, and any prototype, demo, presentation, or supporting materials required for their pitch.

Teams of up to 4

03

E-Summit · Business & Adventure

Fort Knox Escape

Fort Knox Escape is a two-round challenge that combines mystery, strategy, and problem-solving. Round 1 tests analytical thinking through clue analysis and business crisis assessment. Round 2 is an escape-room challenge with riddles, puzzles, coded messages, and hidden clues.

Round 1: Teams identify a mystery company through a series of clues, then analyse a hypothetical business crisis using the information provided. They must present strategic solutions addressing key areas such as sales, marketing, and production.

Round 2: Teams enter an immersive escape-room challenge featuring riddles, puzzles, coded messages, hidden clues, and interactive tasks. Within 2.5 hours, they must communicate, collaborate, manage time, and make strategic decisions to solve the challenges and escape.

The final verdict will be based on analytical thinking, strategic approach, creativity, problem-solving, teamwork, communication, decision-making, and overall performance. In the final round, completion time and adherence to the given rules will also be considered.

The event is open to Classes IX–XII, with four members per team; participants must bring a laptop/tablet for Round 1, with AI tools allowed only for research, not for creating final solutions or presentations. For the escape-room round, organisers will provide all necessary materials, including a torch, notepad, pen, magnifying glass, and other resources required for the challenges.

Teams of 4

04

E-Summit · Sales & Product

Make & Sell

Make & Sell is a live entrepreneurship challenge. Participants develop a product, build its brand identity, and sell it in a live marketplace — turning ideas into real transactions.

The challenge unfolds with Build the Prototype, where teams receive a problem statement or product category and develop an innovative solution using the materials provided. Participants will turn their ideas into tangible prototypes while demonstrating creativity, teamwork, and problem-solving within limited resources.

Once the prototype is ready, teams will develop its brand identity through branding, marketing, and presentation. They will then deliver a short elevator pitch to the judges, highlighting the product's value, vision, and unique features.

The final round, Market & Sell, brings the venture to life as teams operate their assigned stalls, showcase their products, engage with visitors, and encourage pre-orders using event tokens, putting their communication, marketing, persuasion, and selling skills into practice.

The final verdict will be based on innovation and product quality, branding and presentation, effectiveness of the elevator pitch, creativity, teamwork, marketing and communication, customer engagement, and selling ability.

The challenge is open to Classes IX–XII, with 4–5 members per team and one team permitted per school. Teams may bring up to two mobile phones, basic stationery, and decorative materials; a laptop or tablet is optional but recommended.

Teams of 4–5

05

E-Summit · Finance & Trading

Trade Tremors

Trade Tremors is a financial simulation where participants manage firms, make investments, and respond to changing market conditions. The event tests trading knowledge, strategic thinking, decision-making, and crisis management through three rounds of increasing pressure.

The journey begins with Round 1: The New Market Saga, where new WTO guidelines set the stage for the formation of two competing firms — the Paper Lords and the Digital Dons. The Paper Lords enter the market with a starting budget of ₹3,50,000, while the Digital Dons begin with ₹2,00,000. The Digital Dons must pitch their digital currency, market strategy, real-world value, tokenomics, and future vision to attract investments. The Paper Lords then analyse these opportunities and strategically invest in at least two digital firms.

The intensity in Round 2 rises with The Rundown and Ciao Soldi, where participants face unexpected challenges designed to test their adaptability and financial decision-making. While The Rundown introduces a surprise mini-challenge worth up to 25 points, Ciao Soldi places the Paper Lords in a financial crisis, requiring the Digital Dons to step in, evaluate the stock market, and make strategic decisions to keep the profits flowing.

Round 3: 9/11 — Rebound, where teams face a high-pressure financial crisis and must recover significant losses with only limited external support. The Paper Lords must rethink their investment strategies, scrutinize their portfolios, and collaborate with the Digital Dons to develop bonds and generate the funds needed to recover.

The final verdict will be based on trading knowledge, strategic thinking, decision-making, and crisis management.

Teams of up to 5
